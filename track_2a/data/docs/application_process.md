# The Swiss Apprenticeship Application and Interview Process

> Background document for HackApertus Track 2A (FHGR): *AI-powered job-interview coach*.
> Audience: developers building interview flows, interviewer personas and feedback prompts. See `swiss_vet_system.md` for the education system itself (EBA/EFZ, BM, etc.).
> Status: September 2026. Figures are cited with source and year.

---

## 1. Overview: from career choice to the first day

In Switzerland, **young people apply directly to companies**, and the **company chooses its apprentice** (*Lernende/r*). There is no central allocation. The process is much like a real job application, but the candidates are **14–15 years old** and have no work experience. The **Schnupperlehre** (taster apprenticeship) is the central element: it is both career exploration and, very often, part of the selection.

```
2nd year Sek I (10H)       3rd year Sek I (11H)                        August
┌───────────────────┐  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐  ┌──────────────┐
│ Career exploration │  │ Aug: places published → applications     │  │ Apprenticeship│
│ BIZ, fairs,        │→ │ Schnupperlehre (selection) → tests →     │→ │ starts        │
│ Schnupperlehren    │  │ interview(s) → offer → Lehrvertrag       │  │ (Probezeit)   │
└───────────────────┘  └──────────────────────────────────────────┘  └──────────────┘
          ~1 year before the start          the year before the start
```

## 2. Timeline

| When (typical) | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1st Sek year (9H)** / early 2nd | Career-choice lessons at school, interest tests, first BIZ visit, parents' evenings, career fairs (e.g. *Berufsmesse Zürich*, *BAM Bern*, *Salon des métiers* in the Romandie, *Espoprofessioni* in Ticino) |
| **2nd Sek year (10H)** | Core phase of career choice. Explore several occupations and do **Schnupperlehren**. The Zurich *Berufswahlfahrplan* recommends starting them **from January of the 2nd Sek year**. Aptitude tests (Multicheck, Basic-Check) are often taken in the second half of this year or at the beginning of the 3rd year |
| **1 August, one year before the start** | Agreed starting date: open places are **advertised no earlier than August** of the year before the start and published in the cantonal **LENA** (Lehrstellennachweis) on berufsberatung.ch from 1 August |
| **3rd Sek year (11H), Aug–spring** | Applications, selection-Schnupperlehren, interviews. **Lehrverträge are signed no earlier than one year before the start** and **approved by the canton no earlier than September** of the year before the start (commitment of the Confederation, cantons and OdA, reported by the Swiss employers' association in 2021) |
| Spring of the last school year | Most places are taken. In the 2026 Nahtstellenbarometer, **68%** of the approx. 74,000 places offered had already been awarded, and **73%** of young people interested in an apprenticeship had a signed contract (62%) or an oral commitment (11%) (SBFI, June 2026) |
| **August (start)** | Apprenticeship starts, beginning with the **Probezeit** (probation period, 1–3 months) |

In the 2025 survey, 45% of young people experienced the application process as "very positive" and a further 42% as "rather positive" (SBFI, Nahtstellenbarometer 2025).

## 3. The Schnupperlehre (Schnuppertage)

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | A short, unpaid trial stay in a company: **usually 1–5 days** (berufsberatung.ch). FR: *stage (d'information / de découverte)*. IT: *stage (di orientamento)* |
| **Purpose (1): exploration** | The young person checks whether the occupation suits them ("reality check"). Early Schnupperlehren (2nd Sek year) often happen in several occupations |
| **Purpose (2): selection** | Later, a *Selektions-Schnupperlehre* (selection taster) is often a required step. The company observes punctuality, interest, manual skill, questions, behaviour with the team, and often asks for a short report. A **Schnupperbericht** / evaluation sheet is then added to the application dossier |
| **How to get one** | Mostly by **phone call** from the young person themselves (sometimes by e-mail or through an online form). Addresses are available on berufsberatung.ch, on company websites, and through personal networks |
| **After** | A thank-you (letter or e-mail). This is also a good moment to express interest in the apprenticeship place |

### The phone call (from the FHGR material *Unterschiedliche Bewerbungssituationen*)
Goal: show interest, introduce yourself briefly, get an appointment. Structure: greeting with **full name, school and class** → reason for calling (interest in a Schnupperlehre) → ask for possible dates → accept or suggest a date → clarify details (where, when, whom to report to, what to bring, working hours) → thank and say goodbye. Preparation: notes, a quiet place, clear pronunciation. This is a realistic **short scenario** for the coach, e.g. as a warm-up or for EBA candidates.

## 4. The application dossier (Bewerbungsdossier)

According to berufsberatung.ch, a complete dossier contains:

| Document | DE / FR / IT | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cover / motivation letter | Bewerbungsbrief, Motivationsschreiben / lettre de motivation / lettera di motivazione | 1 page. Why this occupation, why this company, what I bring. It should be specific to the company |
| CV | Lebenslauf / curriculum vitae / curriculum vitae | Personal data, school path, Schnupperlehren, languages, hobbies, references. A photo is still common in Switzerland |
| School reports | Zeugnisse (copies) / bulletins scolaires / pagelle | Usually the last 2–3 semester reports of Sek I |
| Schnupperlehre reports | Schnupperberichte / évaluations de stage / valutazioni di stage | Evaluation by the company where the candidate did the Schnupperlehre |
| Tests and checks | see below | Often explicitly required for demanding occupations |
| Other | Arbeitsbestätigungen, certificates, references | E.g. babysitting course, first-aid course, language certificate, club activities |

Applications are mostly sent **by e-mail as a PDF or via online portals**, and less often on paper.

### Tests that appear in dossiers and interviews

| Test | What it is | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Stellwerk** | A **school-internal**, computer-based assessment of school performance (German, maths, French, English, science). It is **not** an aptitude test. It is used in several German-speaking cantons, mainly in the 2nd Sek year | School. Results often end up in the dossier. Interviewers may ask about them (FHGR question bank: "Is there anything in your Stellwerk you'd like to explain?", *only if the candidate has done it*) |
| **Multicheck** | A **commercial aptitude test tailored to occupational groups** (school knowledge plus cognitive skills such as logic, memory, spatial imagination, concentration). The candidate pays a fee | Many companies, especially in technical and commercial occupations. Valued because it is **comparable across cantons** |
| **Basic-Check** | A **commercial aptitude test that is neutral with respect to occupations**, standardised across Switzerland, taken after the 8th school year (10H) | Companies in many sectors |
| Company or association tests | Own tests (e.g. at large companies), sometimes occupation-specific tests from associations | Large companies, banks, public administration, industry |

## 5. Selection steps

Selection steps vary strongly with company size:

| Step | Small company (e.g. carpenter, hairdresser) | Large company (e.g. SBB, Swiss Post, banks, pharma, industry) |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-selection | Dossier, often after a Schnupperlehre | Online application, dossier, often a test score threshold |
| Test | Rarely an own test. Multicheck/Basic-Check result | Own online test and/or Multicheck |
| Schnupperlehre as selection | **Very common, often decisive** | Sometimes (*Schnuppertag*, *Selektionstag*) |
| Interview | 1 interview, owner + trainer | 1–2 interviews, HR + Berufsbildner/in |
| Assessment | Rare | Common: group tasks, role-plays, short presentations |
| Second interview / follow-up | Sometimes with parents present | Possible |
| Decision | Quick, often by phone | Written, sometimes after weeks |

## 6. The typical interview

| Aspect | Typical |
|---|---|
| **Who is present** | The **Berufsbildner/in** (company trainer) and/or the owner or department head. At large companies, someone from **HR**. Sometimes a current apprentice. Occasionally a **parent** accompanies the candidate (more common with small companies or follow-up talks) |
| **Duration** | Usually **30–60 minutes**. The FHGR example interview (Konstrukteur, transcripts `T-SRC-01/02` in [`../transcripts/`](../transcripts/)) announces **about 45 minutes** |
| **Form of address** | In German-speaking Switzerland the interviewer usually **says "du"** to a 15-year-old candidate (as in all FHGR examples). The candidate addresses the adults with **"Sie" + surname** ("Grüezi Frau Meier") unless offered "du". In the Romandie, **"vous"** is used much more consistently in both directions. In Ticino, **"Lei"** is usual towards the interviewer. The coach should make this **configurable per language/persona** |
| **Language** | Swiss German dialect is common in the conversation in German-speaking Switzerland. Written communication is in Standard German. A candidate answering in Standard German is fine |
| **Location** | On site in the company. Increasingly also **online** (Teams/Zoom) |
| **Documents** | The candidate brings a printed dossier, notepad and pen, and prepared questions (FHGR *Allgemeine Vorbereitungen auf ein Vorstellungsgespräch*) |

### 6.1 The FHGR 10-phase flow and the challenge's 6-phase flow

The FHGR source material (*Möglicher Ablauf eines Bewerbungsgesprächs*) describes a 10-phase flow. The challenge description suggests a shorter flow: *introduction → motivation → strengths/weaknesses → situational questions → candidate questions → feedback*. Mapping:

| Challenge phase | FHGR phases | Typical content / example questions (from the FHGR *Beispielfragen*, see [`../questions/`](../questions/)) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | **1 Begrüssung und Einstieg**; **2 Vorstellung** | Small talk ("Hast du den Weg gut gefunden?"), interviewer introduces themselves and explains the flow; "Erzähl uns etwas über dich", school path, hobbies |
| **2. Motivation** | **3 Motivation**; **4 Kenntnisse über Beruf und Betrieb**; **8 Zukunft** | "Warum dieser Beruf?", "Warum bei uns?", "Was weisst du über unseren Betrieb?", tasks and requirements of the occupation, expectations, goals (BM, further training) |
| **3. Strengths / weaknesses** | **5 Persönliche Stärken und Entwicklung**; **6 Schule und Erfahrungen** | Strengths **with an example**, a weakness you are working on, handling mistakes and criticism; favourite subjects, report card / Stellwerk, Schnupperlehre experiences |
| **4. Situational questions** | **7 Soziale Kompetenzen** (+ occupation-specific questions by Berufsfeld) | Teamwork, conflict, criticism. Hypothetical cases ("Was machst du, wenn du einen Fehler bemerkst / eine Kundin unfreundlich ist / du etwas nicht verstehst?"). The FHGR flow has no explicit "situational" phase. Such questions come mainly from phase 7 and from the occupation-specific part of the question bank |
| **5. Candidate questions** | **9 Fragen der Bewerberin / des Bewerbers** | Questions about daily work, the team, the vocational school, BM possibility, next steps. **Having no questions is a weakness** (criterion "Eigeninitiative") |
| **6. Feedback** | **10 Abschluss** (+ coach feedback) | In a real interview: summary, next steps ("Wir melden uns bis …"), goodbye. In the coach: **developmental feedback** according to the FHGR feedback criteria ([feedback_guidelines.md](feedback_guidelines.md)) (strengths first, 1–2 points per criterion, concrete next step, encouraging end) |

> Note: the real closing phase (phase 10) gives **no evaluation**. Feedback is a training feature of the coach. It makes sense to separate the two: the interviewer persona closes realistically, and then a "coach" voice gives feedback.

### 6.2 Evaluation criteria (FHGR)
FHGR evaluates answers along **11 criteria**: clarity, relevance, motivation, self-reflection, communication, concrete examples, appearance and impact, preparation/knowledge of the company, goal orientation, handling difficult questions, and initiative. Each has a four-level scale (*ungenügend / ausbaufähig / gut / sehr gut*) (see [`../rubric/criteria.json`](../rubric/criteria.json) and [feedback_guidelines.md](feedback_guidelines.md)).

## 7. The different application situations

Based on the FHGR material *Unterschiedliche Bewerbungssituationen* and *Allgemeine Vorbereitungen auf ein Vorstellungsgespräch* (see also [interview_situations.md](interview_situations.md)):

| Situation | Goal / focus | Formality | Special points for the simulation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phone call** (asking for a Schnupperlehre) | Introduce yourself, show interest, get a date | Low–medium | Short (2–5 min). Full name, school, class. Polite, clear speech. Well suited to TTS |
| **Information or getting-to-know talk** | Get to know the occupation or company, ask questions | Low | The **candidate asks** more. Often during career exploration |
| **Schnupperlehre talk** | Motivation, interest, reliability, expectations | Medium | Often the first formal talk. Shorter than an apprenticeship interview |
| **Online interview** | Like the on-site interview, plus media skills | High | Look into the camera, test the technology, quiet background, log in 5–10 min early, notifications off |
| **Apprenticeship interview** (*Vorstellungsgespräch*) | Choosing a suitable apprentice | High | The "classic" case. Full 10-phase flow |
| **Assessment** (structured selection) | Interview plus role-plays, group tasks, case studies, short presentations | High | Mainly at large companies. **The process counts as much as the result**: listening, asking back, cooperation |
| **Follow-up / second interview** | After the Schnupperlehre or first interview: exchange impressions, clarify open points, decide | Medium–high | Refers to concrete Schnupperlehre experiences. Parents may be present |

## 8. What companies look for

The FHGR criteria and practice guidance point to the same things:

- **Genuine motivation for the occupation *and* the company**, based on concrete experience (Schnupperlehre), not on what parents said.
- **Reliability and work attitude**: punctuality, politeness, following instructions, the "Arbeitshaltung" grades in the report card.
- **Willingness to learn and handling of mistakes/criticism**: very important, because the company invests 2–4 years in training.
- **Basic school skills**: in the 2025 Nahtstellenbarometer, more than a third of companies reported receiving applications from unsuitable candidates, mainly because of **insufficient school performance and language deficits** (SBFI, Oct 2025).
- **Team fit and manners** with adults, colleagues and customers.
- **Preparation**: knowing the company, the occupation's tasks, and having **own questions**. The FHGR material notes that the interview "is not a career information session".
- **Fit with the occupation's demands** (e.g. manual skill, spatial imagination, contact with people), often checked in the Schnupperlehre.

## 9. Offer, Lehrvertrag and cantonal approval

1. **Offer** (*Zusage*): often by phone first, then in writing. The candidate should answer within the agreed time and **inform other companies** if they accept an offer elsewhere.
2. **Lehrvertrag** (*contrat d'apprentissage* / *contratto di tirocinio*): uses the official national form. It is signed by the company, the apprentice, and **the legal representative (parents)** if the apprentice is a minor. It contains the occupation, duration, probation period, apprentice wage, working hours, holidays, and any BM1.
3. **Cantonal approval**: the company submits the contract to the **cantonal VET office** before the start. The office checks it and approves it (BBG Art. 14), no earlier than September of the year before the start.
4. **Probation period**: 1–3 months, extendable to a maximum of 6 months with approval from the canton. During this time either side can end the contract at short notice.

## 10. Differences in the Romandie and Ticino

| Aspect | German-speaking Switzerland | Romandie (FR) | Ticino (IT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key terms | Lehre, Lehrstelle, Schnupperlehre, EFZ/EBA, BM | apprentissage, place d'apprentissage, **stage**, CFC/AFP, MP | tirocinio, posto di tirocinio, **stage**, AFC/CFP, MP |
| School-based VET | Less common | **More common** (écoles de métiers, écoles de commerce) | **More common** (e.g. scuole professionali a tempo pieno) |
| Last school year | "3. Sek" / 9th year / 11H | **11H** (cycle d'orientation / secondaire I) | **4a media** (4-year scuola media) |
| Guidance | BIZ | orientation scolaire et professionnelle (OSP), cantonal offices | Ufficio dell'orientamento scolastico e professionale (UOSP) |
| Form of address in interview | Often "du" to the candidate | Mostly **"vous"** | Mostly **"Lei"** towards the adult; candidates are also often addressed with "tu" |
| Language of conversation | Dialect is common | Standard French | Italian (dialect less relevant for the interview) |
| Tests | Stellwerk (school), Multicheck, Basic-Check | Multicheck/Basic-Check also available; some cantons have their own tests | Varies; company tests |
| Bridge offers | 10. Schuljahr, Vorlehre, SEMO | solutions transitoires, préapprentissage, SEMO | pretirocinio di orientamento / d'integrazione |

The overall process is the same nationwide: a federal VET law, federal certificates, and the same Lehrvertrag logic.

## 11. Common pitfalls of young applicants (from the FHGR material)

Derived from the FHGR *Kriterienraster*, the Konstrukteur example interview, the feedback criteria and *Allgemeine Vorbereitungen auf ein Vorstellungsgespräch*:

| Pitfall | Weak example (FHGR) | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Monosyllabic answers, no structure | "Ja ok." / "Äh, ich bin 15 und gehe zur Schule." | Short introduction: school, interests, link to the occupation |
| Motivation comes from outside | "Meine Eltern haben gesagt, das sei ein guter Beruf mit Zukunft." | Own experience: "In der Schnupperlehre habe ich gemerkt, …" |
| Claim without example | "Keine Ahnung, ich denke, dass ich genau arbeiten kann." | Strength plus a concrete situation (situation → action → result) |
| No knowledge of the company | Generic phrases ("ein cooler Betrieb") | Specific facts from the website or the Schnupperlehre |
| Treating the interview as an information session | Asking basic questions about what the job is | Know the occupation beforehand. Ask deeper questions |
| No own questions | "Nein, ich habe keine Fragen." | 2–3 prepared questions (daily work, team, vocational school, BM) |
| Too informal | "Ja, hoi. War ok, hab's gefunden." (looking at the floor) | "Grüezi Frau Meier, danke für die Einladung." Eye contact |
| Distracted / impolite | Phone visible, chewing gum, cap or headphones | Phone silent and in the bag |
| Hobbies without relevance | "Eigentlich nichts Spezielles. Am Handy sein, so." | Hobby plus what it taught you (team spirit, reliability) |
| Weakness as a disguised strength / no reflection | "Ich bin zu perfektionistisch." or "Ich habe keine Schwächen." | A real, harmless weakness plus what you are doing about it |
| Online: technology not tested | Late, bad sound, looking at the screen | Test beforehand, log in 5–10 min early, look into the camera |

**Not pitfalls:** nervousness, slips of the tongue and pauses are normal in a first interview and **should not be penalised** (FHGR feedback guidelines).

## 12. Implications for the interview coach

1. **Model the right scenario.** Support at least the classic **apprenticeship interview**. Add the **Schnupperlehre talk**, the **phone call** and the **online interview** as lighter or specialised variants (they suit TTS and short sessions). The assessment is hard to simulate. A single role-play or case task is a realistic subset.
2. **Use the 6-phase flow, fed by the 10 FHGR phases** (table in section 6.1). Keep the realistic closing ("Wir melden uns bis Ende nächster Woche") separate from the **coach feedback**.
3. **The interviewer persona should be a Swiss Berufsbildner/in**, not a corporate recruiter. Warm but assessing. Says "du" (DE), "vous" (FR), and "Lei" or "tu" by configuration (IT). Short questions, one at a time, 8–15 main questions for a 30–45-minute interview, with follow-up questions when an answer lacks an example.
4. **Personalise with Swiss artefacts.** Build the candidate profile from occupation (Berufsfeld, EBA/EFZ, BM1 yes/no), Sek level, Schnupperlehren done, Stellwerk/Multicheck results, and hobbies. Company profiles should be small or large Swiss companies with realistic selection steps (section 5).
5. **Ask about what the candidate actually has:** Schnupperlehre experiences, school projects, club activities, family duties. Do not ask about previous jobs, salary expectations or career plans for the next 10 years.
6. **Allowed and expected Swiss questions:** Zeugnis, Stellwerk (only if done), Multicheck, Sek level, BM plans, commute. Do **not** ask about religion, origin, family finances or health (except occupation-relevant requirements, e.g. allergies for a baker or hairdresser, and only neutrally).
7. **Timing awareness:** a candidate in the 2nd Sek year is exploring (Schnupperlehre talk). A candidate in the 3rd year between August and spring is applying (interview). After spring, the remaining places and bridge offers are the relevant context.
8. **Feedback anchored in FHGR criteria:** use the 11 criteria and four levels. Quote the candidate. Start with strengths. Give 1–2 improvement points and a concrete next step. Use age-appropriate language. Never create fear. When a candidate is very insecure, refer them to real people (BIZ counsellor, teacher, parents).
9. **Language hygiene:** use Swiss terms (Lehrstelle, Lernende/r, Berufsbildner/in, Schnupperlehre, Lehrvertrag; FR stage/apprenti/CFC; IT stage/apprendista/AFC), and "ss" instead of "ß". Avoid German, French or Italian national concepts (Ausbildungsplatz/IHK/Azubi, alternance/bac pro, istituto tecnico).
